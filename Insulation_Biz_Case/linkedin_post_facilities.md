If you're planning fabric and battery rollouts as separate workstreams, this changes the order.

A post-retrofit home needs just 26% of a standard battery's capacity to cover the full evening peak. An unretrofitted one needs 98% — barely enough, no headroom. Same battery, same peak window, completely different margin.

Retrofit first, and "adequate battery provision" for everything that follows means something smaller and cheaper than it would otherwise.

The same physics shows up as avoided peak demand: 2.46 kW/home down to 0.66 kW/home — 1.8 kW/home, or 1.8 MW per 1,000 homes retrofitted. Real headroom for a DNO conversation about local network capacity or EV charging rollout.

Two spec notes worth carrying into any programme, both real but neither quantified for this specific archetype yet: external wall insulation plausibly beats internal on summer comfort (CIBSE TM59) — and solar added at scale on one street risks tripping LV voltage limits at exactly the times it's most likely to export, worth raising with the DNO before phasing street-by-street rather than after.
