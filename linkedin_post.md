If you plan network capacity or procure flexibility on UK distribution networks: insulating a pre-1919 solid-wall terrace to EPC-C cuts steady-state heat-pump peak demand by 1.8 kW/home — 1.8 MW per 1,000 homes retrofitted — and lets it coast through a 4-hour evening peak with heating off (11.4–14.3 hours, checked with a two-node thermal model). Unretrofitted fabric doesn't reliably clear that same window at all (1.6–3.9 hours) — don't bank on it.

The catch: only 27% of a real, mixed-tenure estate has this today, using actual English Housing Survey tenure and insulation data — and it's uneven. A specific street can range from 0% to 37% insulated, Right to Buy fragmentation puts a chunk of any estate outside a landlord's reach entirely, and the capacity that does exist shrinks fastest exactly when a cold snap gets worst.

It's also not something you can buy yet: passive fabric has no meter or dispatch signal, so it can't bid into a flexibility tender the way a battery can. The real value sits in avoided reinforcement capex and bill savings — not quantified here, and where I'd like to take this next with real feeder data.
